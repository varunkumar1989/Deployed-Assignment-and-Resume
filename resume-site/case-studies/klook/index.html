Klook: booking a full-day speedboat excursion

A multi-option booking flow with dates, transport tiers, group size and dynamic pricing. High cognitive load makes it a good test of hierarchy and error prevention.

Findings

#1Package choice paralysis

Early and late departures and group offers are clustered with poor hierarchy, creating a dense wall of choices.

Principle
Hick's Law; aesthetic and minimalist design
Fix
Use progressive disclosure: Step 1 choose time, Step 2 choose transfer.

#2Micro touch targets

The plus and minus adjusters for adults and children are tiny text icons spaced tightly.

Principle
Fitts's Law
Fix
Make targets at least 48 by 48px with a grey fill on each icon.

#3Dead-end promo errors

An invalid code shows a generic dark toast: “Invalid promo code. Please double check.”

Principle
Help users recognize, diagnose and recover from errors
Fix
Say why (expired, minimum spend not met) and suggest other coupons.

#4Chaotic pickup mapping

Overlapping semi-transparent polygons with floating price tags make it hard to verify the hotel zone.

Principle
Visibility of system status; match with the real world
Fix
Replace the map with address autocomplete that shows the surcharge in a text banner.

#5Intrusive upsell banner

A bright Satisfaction Guarantee banner right before payment adds visual noise.

Principle
Aesthetic and minimalist design
Fix
Condense it into a single-line toggle in the price breakdown.

Redesign direction

Structural clarityA step-wise configuration wizard that isolates high-stakes choices.
Interaction engineeringLarger tap boundaries on all primary configuration controls.
Logistics simplificationAddress validation that says whether free pickup applies.
Intelligent error handlingContextual guidance instead of static dead-end pop-ups.
